# Mineral — Style Guide & System Notes

*a companion for the creative psyche*

v1 · May 2026

---

## What Mineral is

A daily practice app that walks alongside creatives reckoning with their creative callings. The work draws on Integral and Transpersonal Psychology, Aperspectival structures of consciousness, and arts-based research.

Each day is an **encounter** — a short voice guide, a reflection, and a small integration practice. Over time, the user's responses become **field notes**, which the system reflects back as a **field guide** — a living mirror of patterns moving through their creative psyche.

The architecture is fractal: the same shape — four cardinal positions on the spiral around an integral still point — appears at three scales. A daily encounter sits inside a phase of the journey, which sits inside the user's 28-year life cycle. One geometry, learned once, deepens.

The product is not a coaching program, a creativity course, or a productivity system. It is a place to enter daily to reconnect with oneself.

## Eight commitments

These are the principles that govern every design and content decision in Mineral. Refer to them when in doubt.

### 1. The engine counts; it does not interpret.
Frequencies, direct quotes, distributions. Never "your clearest signals" or "blocks that ask to be seen." The user's pattern is the user's to read.

### 2. Meaning-making belongs to the user.
Wider context appears only in "from the field" offerings — visually distinct, plain-voiced, plural in possibility, always located in others' observations ("has been read as") rather than diagnoses.

### 3. One block per screen.
The encounter is a sequence of block-screens, not one scroll — advancing by tap, one gesture per screen. Length varies by encounter (typically 5–7 screens, plus an optional deep-dive branch). Expansive, ritualistic, no dashboard density. Tabs disappear inside the encounter; so do progress indicators. The encounter is a held space, not a process bar.

### 3a. Capture is the center, not the destination.
The crystallizing capture comes early and is unmissable — it opens the post-audio experience as its centerpiece, never buried at the end of a corridor. Everything else (practice, integration, deep dive) is depth arranged *around* that central capture, not gates in front of it. The user's ability to see her own pattern through her captured responses is the most valuable thing the product does; the design protects the capture moment above all else.

### 4. Voice-first capture.
Capture defaults to hold-to-record in both the encounter and the Notes tab. Text is secondary; the user speaks, the app receives. The crystallizing capture (the ⟡ response) is the protected center of each encounter; ambient captures are available throughout for whatever else lands.

### 5. Fractal geometry, one shape.
Four cardinal Aperspectival structures plus integral center, repeated at three scales: 28-year life cycle, the journey through four phases, today's encounter. Learned once, deepens.

### 6. The user's voice is primary.
Visual hierarchy enforces this. User quotes in serif italic against colored borders. Engine in synthesis sentences. Offerings in lower-opacity sans-serif containers.

### 7. Optional everywhere it can be.
Centering, reflections, integration, Human Design, offerings. The encounter has shape but no gates. Users go as deep as the day allows.

### 8. Atmosphere is descriptive, not prescriptive.
Today is mythical because the journey says so, not because the engine thinks the user needs mythical content. Calendar facts only.

## The encounter flow

The encounter is the heart of the practice. It is not a fixed set of screens — it is a sequence of typed blocks, each rendered as one screen, advancing by tap. The user moves through at her own pace; she can set the phone down mid-practice and return; she can leave entirely and resume where she left off.

### Block types

| Block | Screen behavior | Capture |
|-------|-----------------|---------|
| **listen** | Audio playback. Controls fade so she can be with the voice, not the UI. | No |
| **crystallizing capture** | The centerpiece. The ⟡ prompt, large, with the record affordance front and center. Opens the post-audio experience. | **Primary** — auto-typed `reflection` |
| **practice** | Guided somatic/embodied steps (posture, breath, visualization). Text only in v1. Follow-along, no rush. | Ambient affordance available |
| **reflection** | Prompts as invitation, building toward depth. May be warm-up (no ⟡) or hold the ⟡ itself. | Ambient; primary if ⟡ here |
| **integration** | One anchoring action, occasionally two. The action happens in her life, not the app. | Ambient; primary if ⟡ here |
| **carry** | The soft close. A held thought — what to notice as the day unfolds. | No |
| **deep dive** | Optional branch before the close. Its own block sequence and its own ⟡. Offered, never forced. | Its own crystallizing capture + ambient |

### Two kinds of capture

**The crystallizing capture** is an *event*. It comes early (it opens the post-audio experience), it is the centerpiece of its screen, and it is the response the whole encounter was built to evoke. Auto-typed `reflection` — frictionless, she just responds to the ⟡ prompt. This is the anchored field note, weighted most heavily in the Guide. The supporting reflection prompts are available on this screen (below, or via a "need a way in?" reveal) to warm her up, but she can record immediately if she's ready. This capture is structurally guaranteed — every user who does the encounter meets it first.

**Ambient captures** are an *affordance*. As she moves through practice, integration, and deep dive, a quiet persistent capture control is always in reach. Anytime something lands, she taps, keeps it, and continues exactly where she was. These reuse the same capture sheet as the Notes tab, including the nine-chip type-picker — so an in-encounter spark is typed and stored identically to a spontaneous one from Notes. Ambient captures are pure upside: they enrich the field and the Guide's lenses, but never gate anything. A user who captures once loses nothing; a user who captures three times gives the Guide richer material.

All captures from one encounter share the same `encounterRef`. The crystallizing one is distinguished by carrying the ⟡ prompt's `questionId` and is weighted accordingly; ambient ones carry their user-chosen type. The schema (six-collection model) already supports multiple field notes per encounter — no change needed.

### Navigation

- **Forward:** tap "continue →" / "when you're ready →" in the footer. Practice screens use unhurried language ("when you're ready") and never pressure forward motion — these screens are comfortable being set down.
- **Backward:** a quiet back affordance (subtle ‹ or swipe-right) for re-reading. Recovery, not a primary path. The encounter flows forward.
- **Exit and resume:** leaving mid-encounter saves an `in-progress` state with the block index. Today shows "continue your encounter →" rather than restarting. Encounters are long enough that one-sitting completion can't be assumed.
- **No progress dots inside the encounter.** It is a held space, not a process bar. She advances when ready; she does not watch herself approach an end.
- **Deep dive** is offered as a quiet affordance after integration, before carry: *"there's more here, if you have time →"*. Tap to enter; skip to close. Never forced.

## The four phases

The user walks four cardinal positions on the spiral. Each turn deepens. Phase V — The Next Turn — is not a destination within the turn; it is the next turn itself, where everything prior integrates and the spiral begins again at greater depth.

### Phase I — The Signal
**North · Archaic · #C44A8A**
*Something is calling.*
Themes: longing, identity, resonance, lineage, permission, vocation, devotion.
Tone: awakening, magnetic, intimate.

### Phase II — The Field
**East · Magical · #5DCAA5**
*Learn the ecology of your creativity.*
Themes: energy, environment, inspiration, rhythm, emotional weather, resistance, synchronicity.
Tone: curious, observant, exploratory.

### Phase III — The Friction
**South · Mythical · #D89A3A**
*The work meets resistance.*
Themes: perfectionism, fear of visibility, fragmentation, ambition, control, burnout, grief — balanced with beauty, devotion, breakthroughs, flow.
Tone: honest, transformational, resilient.

### Phase IV — The Voice
**West · Mental · #6B8EB8**
*Expression becomes embodiment.*
Themes: communication, leadership, visibility, relationships, contribution, creative authority, magnetism.
Tone: alive, expressive, relational.

### Phase V — The Next Turn
**Center · Integral · #A89CDC**
*You become the environment.*
Themes: integration, mentorship, generosity, sustainability, legacy, stewardship, living the work.
Tone: spacious, grounded, luminous.

Phase V is not a standalone phase within the turn — it is the moment of crossing into the next revolution of the spiral, carrying the integration of all four prior phases. The user does not "arrive" at The Next Turn; they discover they are already living it as The Signal begins to call again, differently.

**The spiral continues.** Through Phase V, the user returns to The Signal — but at a deeper turn. The spiral does not end. Tempo is not enforced by calendar; the engine paces by depth of engagement, not days elapsed.

## The spiral life map

The Origin tab renders the same four-cardinal geometry at the largest scale: a life, drawn as a spiral. This section is canon for how that scale works — derived from the original hand-drawn life map.

**The revolution is 28 years, in four 7-year quarters.** Crossings are anchored to the birthday: the user crosses a cardinal every 7th birthday (7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56…). The 28th birthday is the first Return; the spiral continues into a second revolution, wider.

**The life scale has its own quarter names**, paired with the consciousness structures:

| Cardinal | Life-scale name | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| North | Origin | Unitive / Archaic |
| East | Dance of Opposites | Magical |
| South | Max Diversity | Mythical |
| West | Return to Wholeness | Mental |
| North (return) | The Return | Unitive — origin and integral are the same point, met at different turns |

The practice scale keeps Signal / Field / Friction / Voice. Scale-native naming is deliberate: same geometry, different vocabulary per zoom level.

**The fractal is geometric, not numeric.** Four cardinals, a still point, and return-at-depth repeat at every scale — but each scale keeps its native rhythm: **7 years per quarter** at life scale, **27 days per phase** at practice scale (a 108-day turn). Never force one scale's numbers onto another. (Engineering note: the cycle math in `LifeMapSpiral` stays at 28. An earlier suggestion to change it to 27 is retracted — the life map is the authority.)

**The pendulum is the NOW-pointer at every zoom.** On the life map it is a fine needle from the still point to today's position on the arc. On the Today screen it is the centering pendulum. Same object, different scale.

**Crossing dates are computed, and shown descriptively.** From birth date alone (birth time and location feed Human Design, not the spiral math), the client can compute every crossing — e.g. a meta row reading *"crossed into Return to Wholeness · May 20, 2024."* Descriptive, factual, quietly mythic. No schema change; it is client-side arithmetic on data already collected.

**Timing, not progress.** No spiral surface — Origin, Today, or any future zoom level — ever shows completion percentage, streaks, or progress bars. The spiral shows *where you are in a rhythm*, never how much you have consumed. This sentence is load-bearing; it is the difference between a cosmology and a dashboard.

**Roadmap: zoom is the interaction model.** The long-term Origin experience is one continuous zoom — life map → current 108-day turn → today's encounter (the Personal Spiral Generation direction). v1 renders the life map statically, built as the outermost frame of that future stack.

## Typography

Two registers, one system.

### Sans family
A geometric humanist sans with a strong personality. Suggested: **Söhne**, **GT Walsheim**, or **Inter**. Avoid generic system sans for production.

### Serif family
A serif with a meaningful italic. Suggested: **GT Sectra**, **Tiempos Headline**, or **Iowan Old Style** in italic. The italic must feel personal, not decorative.

### Weight rules
- Body and titles: **500 (medium)** — never bold
- Heaviness through scale and contrast, not weight
- Serif italic: always **400**

### Case rules
- Titles and headers: **sentence case**
- Tab labels and navigation: **lowercase**
- Eyebrows and small metadata: **UPPERCASE with wide tracking** (0.22em)

## Voice & writing

### Engine voice (Guide synthesis)
- **Counts only.** "Water appears in 6 signals." Not "your clearest signals."
- **Direct quotes with attribution.** "The door doesn't open until I admit I want to walk through." — reflection · the threshold · 3 days ago
- **No causal claims.** "Noted in 8 of 9 sparks" — not "+84% charge."
- **Never names what the user is doing.** No "your visibility fear" — only "visibility fear" as a category the user named.

### "From the field" voice
Wider cultural context appears inline as optional offerings. Plain-voiced, plural in possibility, distanced from the user.

**Rules:**
- Use passive locating constructions: "has been read as," "has been associated with"
- Plural rather than singular: "often guarded" not "is guarded"
- Never name what *this user* is doing — only what others have observed about the image
- Sans-serif, lower opacity, in a soft container — visually subordinate to user quotes

**Example offerings:**

> **the threshold:** In mythic literature, the threshold has been read as the moment of crossing — where one identity ends and another begins. It appears in initiation stories across cultures, often guarded.

> **visibility fear:** Many creative practitioners describe visibility as something both desired and resisted. The fear has been named the price of being seen; the work has been named what makes the price worth paying.

> **dawn walks:** Walking has long been associated with the unblocking of creative thought. The body's rhythm, peripheral attention, and changing scenery have been linked to lateral connections that don't surface in sitting.

> **water:** Water has been read as a symbol of the unconscious, of emotion, of transition. In dreams, currents and depths are often noted as carrying information the waking mind has not yet held.

## Past encounters — access model

**Can users swipe back to listen to past encounters?**

Yes — and this is worth building intentionally. The question is *where* it lives and *how* it's framed.

**The case for it.** Past encounters are part of the user's field. Returning to "Body Before Mind" six weeks in and hearing it differently is itself a practice — the encounter hasn't changed, but the user has. It's also a natural re-entry point when someone has missed several days and doesn't want to feel behind.

**Where it should live.** Not in the main Today flow — that screen should always feel present-tense. The right location is a **quiet affordance at the bottom of the Today screen**, below the current encounter and below the fold. After the current encounter is complete, a soft link appears: *what you've walked through →* leading to a simple reverse-chronological list.

**Framing matters.** It should not be called a "library" or "archive" — both imply passive storage. Better language: *"what you've walked through"* or *"past encounters."* The list belongs to the user, not a catalog.

**What the detail view shows on revisit.** The original audio, the reflection questions — and crucially, the field notes that emerged from that encounter. "When you listened to The Mirror, you noted: [captures from that day]." This connects the archived encounter back to the living field. Returning is not nostalgic — it's relational.

**Implementation.** In v1, a simple list accessible from the bottom of Today post-encounter. In v2, surface contextually in Guide: which past encounters produced the most charged field notes, shown alongside recurring patterns.

## Auth placement

**Where account creation occurs in the onboarding flow.**

Account creation occurs after The Threshold (Begin) screen and *before* the first encounter begins. The user has seen the encounter they're about to enter, felt the pull of it, and then creates an account to enter it.

This is a deliberate **"auth-after-desire"** placement — distinct from both auth-as-gate (account creation at the very start, before the user has seen anything) and auth-after-experience (account creation after the first encounter completes).

**Why this placement:**

The user has walked through five onboarding screens by the time they reach The Threshold. They have seen the practice, the spiral, their starting position, and the encounter they are about to enter. The account ask lands when they are most motivated to begin — they want to walk through this door — and least likely to abandon, because the alternative is to stop now after having invested attention.

It also protects the user's first reflection from being lost. If account creation occurred *after* the first encounter, the user's voice recording would either need to be stored locally and synced later (a non-trivial technical pattern) or risk being lost if the user abandons before creating the account.

**Why not auth-as-gate:**

Front-loading account creation — before the user has seen the spiral, the practice, or any sense of what Mineral is — is the conventional pattern but a weak one for a contemplative practice. It asks for commitment before establishing value. Users abandon at this screen at the highest rates in the funnel. Mineral does not begin with a transaction.

**Why not auth-after-experience:**

Letting the user complete the first encounter without an account is the most user-respectful placement — they get the full felt experience before any ask. But it requires local-first storage with sync-on-auth, which is meaningfully more complex to implement. In v1 the technical cost outweighs the benefit. This placement is held as a v1.1 consideration if abandonment rates at the current auth screen turn out to be high.

**Voice and copy:**

The auth screen makes the practical case plainly: *"your field notes are tied to your account."* No marketing language, no list of benefits. The user creates an account because the practice requires somewhere to keep what they say. Email and password fields; no social login in v1.

**To revisit:**

If post-launch data shows the current auth placement creates significant abandonment, move account creation to *after* the first encounter completes, with local-first reflection storage. This v1.1 path is the user-respectful version and worth the engineering work once there is evidence that the current pattern is creating friction.

## Engineering brief

### Content data model

**Encounter**: title, subtitle, audio file, phase assignment, reflection questions (1–3, all optional), integration practice, optional "from the field" notes on motifs likely to appear.

**Field note**: type (one of 9 chips), content (text or audio), timestamp, atmosphere/phase captured in, source (spontaneous vs. from-encounter).

**From-the-field offering**: keyed to a motif, resistance, or condition. Plain-voiced, plural in possibility, never names the user.

### Pattern engine rules

- **Counts only**: frequency of motifs, recurring phrases, signal type distribution.
- **Never infer**: causality, charge derivation (from content sentiment, not user-rated), psychological diagnoses.
- **Quote verbatim**: when surfacing user words in Guide, use exact phrasing with full attribution (source + date).
- **Real-time**: synthesis sentence updates each new signal; freshness moment is post-encounter only.

### Animation specs

| Animation | Spec |
|-----------|------|
| Atmosphere shifts | Background gradient cross-fades over ~1.5s. Subtle, not announcing itself. |
| Pendulum centering | On tap of still point: cardinal markers fade to 25% opacity over 600ms; pendulum bob settles to center with damped oscillation over ~2s; instruction text fades in at 1s. |
| Taking root | On post-encounter Guide landing: eyebrow + pulse fade in (200ms); synthesis sentence at 600ms; fresh fragment underline draws left-to-right at 1.4s ease-out; trace panel at 1s; explore rows at 1.6s. |
| Today spiral | Current position has subtle 3s pulse — outer ring expanding from 100% to 110% radius and back, opacity 50→20→50. |

### Shared components

Mineral has a small set of shared visual primitives that every screen must use rather than reimplement inline. This is non-negotiable — drift between inline implementations produces subtle visual differences that compound across screens and are expensive to fix later.

| Component | Location | Purpose |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| `<ArchaicAtmosphere />` (and four phase variants — see below) | `components/Atmosphere.tsx` | The radial-gradient background. One file, five phase variants. |
| `<SpiralIndicator phase turn />` | `components/SpiralComponents.tsx` | The small four-cardinal compass used on the Today tab. |
| `<OnboardingSpiral />` | `components/SpiralComponents.tsx` | The larger spiral used on the onboarding wheel screen. |
| `<LifeMapSpiral birthYear currentYear />` | `components/SpiralComponents.tsx` | The 28-year wheel used on the Origin tab. |
| `<OriginMeta birthYear humanDesignType />` | `components/SpiralComponents.tsx` | The metadata rows beneath the life map. |
| `<OnboardingFooter activeStep totalSteps />` | `components/OnboardingFooter.tsx` | The progress dots + continue button at the bottom of onboarding screens. |
| `<BeginButton phase minutes onPress />` | `components/BeginButton.tsx` | The near-white pill with phase-tinted play circle, used to begin any encounter. |

**Screens MUST NOT define their own RadialGradient, LinearGradient, or any other atmospheric background inline.** This rule was established after early drift produced multiple inline implementations that looked subtly different from each other across screens. If a screen needs a new atmosphere or component variant, extend the shared component — don't bypass it.

To verify the rule holds, periodically run `grep -rn "RadialGradient" .` — there should be exactly ONE file defining a RadialGradient in the entire codebase.

### Atmosphere components — naming convention

The atmospheric background has five phase variants, named for the Aperspectival structure each one represents. All five live in the same file (`components/Atmosphere.tsx`) and share a single factory function that takes color stops as a prop. Only the colors change between them; the gradient logic, positioning, and falloff are identical across all five.

| Component | Phase | Position on spiral | Tone |
|-----------|-------|---------------------|------|
| `ArchaicAtmosphere` | Phase I — The Signal | North | Deep aubergine / pink-glow |
| `MagicalAtmosphere` | Phase II — The Field | East | Deep forest / mint-glow |
| `MythicalAtmosphere` | Phase III — The Friction | South | Deep ember / amber-glow |
| `MentalAtmosphere` | Phase IV — The Voice | West | Deep slate / blue-glow |
| `IntegralAtmosphere` | Phase V — The Next Turn | Center / still point | Deep indigo / lavender-glow |

**How screens select their atmosphere.** Encounter screens, reflection screens, and integrate screens use the atmosphere of the encounter's phase. The Today tab uses the atmosphere of the user's current journey phase. Onboarding screens always use `ArchaicAtmosphere` since the user is entering at The Signal. The Origin tab uses `IntegralAtmosphere` because the spiral life map lives at the still point. Notes and Guide tabs use the atmosphere of the user's current journey phase, matching Today.

**Transition behavior.** When the user advances between phases, the atmosphere cross-fades over ~1.5 seconds (per Animation specs above). The shift is subtle, not announcing itself — it's how time feels different in The Friction than in The Signal.

**Pattern for phase-driven rendering:**

```tsx
const ATMOSPHERE_BY_PHASE = {
  signal:   ArchaicAtmosphere,
  field:    MagicalAtmosphere,
  friction: MythicalAtmosphere,
  voice:    MentalAtmosphere,
  next:     IntegralAtmosphere,
};

function EncounterScreen({ encounter }) {
  const Atmosphere = ATMOSPHERE_BY_PHASE[encounter.phase];
  return (
    <View>
      <Atmosphere />
      {/* ... */}
    </View>
  );
}
```

This is the canonical pattern for any screen whose atmosphere depends on phase. Resist the urge to write conditional logic inside a single atmosphere component — keep the components separate and pick the right one at render time.
